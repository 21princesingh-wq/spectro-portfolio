01 · Introduction

Why Build a
Spectrophotometer?

Conventional laboratory-grade spectrophotometers are essential tools for material analysis, but their high cost, large size, and non-portable nature significantly restrict their use in field applications, educational laboratories, and industrial quality control settings.

"Every material possesses a unique reflectance spectrum — a chemical and physical fingerprint that enables accurate, non-destructive identification."

This project proposes a low-cost, portable Reflectance Spectrophotometer using a common camera and diffraction grating to capture visible light spectra (400–700 nm). RGB pixel intensities are mapped mathematically to wavelengths, and a Deep Learning model classifies materials in real-time through a user-friendly GUI.

⚡ Challenge: LED Intensity Uniformity

Achieving stable, consistent illumination with low-cost LEDs is difficult. Inconsistent brightness causes varying reflectance measurements, directly impacting spectral curve accuracy and DL training data quality.

Solution: Trials with different LED configurations controlled via Arduino PWM until uniform illumination is achieved.

📐 Challenge: Camera Angle Alignment

The exact angle between sample, diffraction element, and camera is highly sensitive. An improper angle captures only a partial spectrum, preventing full 400–700nm mapping.

Solution: Iterative mechanical adjustment with calibration against known atomic spectral lines.

06 · Future Scope

Phase II & Beyond:
The Road Ahead

Phase II · Immediate

RGB-to-Wavelength Calibration Engine

Develop and validate the core mapping algorithm using known atomic spectral lines. Achieve reliable, repeatable calibration across different camera settings.

Phase II · Short-Term

Deep Learning Model Training

Collect and label a pilot dataset of 10–15 material samples. Train and validate a CNN/LSTM classifier with target accuracy above 90%.

Phase II · Short-Term

Full GUI Integration

Integrate the DL classifier with real-time spectral curve generation in a Tkinter/PyQt GUI. Enable one-click material prediction with confidence scores.

Phase III · Medium-Term

Spectral Reference Library

Build a comprehensive reference database of material spectra to enable automatic matching without retraining — expanding identifiable materials significantly.

Phase III · Long-Term

Transfer Learning & Generalization

Apply transfer learning to adapt the classifier to new material categories with minimal additional data, improving scalability across industrial domains.

Smartphone Integration

Port the system to Android/iOS, enabling truly field-deployable spectral analysis with a mobile diffraction attachment and on-device inference.
